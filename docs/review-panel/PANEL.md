# The review panel: five gamers from 2004

6 Oct 2026. Tom's brief: score the latest build for how fun it is, against what great games of the era had, and say what would make it an Aylmer cult classic. This file is who scores it and how. `METHOD.md` is how the scoring is run, and `tools/review/harness.mjs` produces the evidence everyone scores from.

**Why personas at all.** "Is it fun?" has no single answer. A 14-year-old on a GameCube and a 28-year-old who reads PC Gamer cover to cover want different things from the same game, and a game becomes a cult classic when it wins one small group completely, not when it averages well. So the panel is five specific people, each with their own weights, and the report keeps their disagreements visible instead of averaging them away.

**The year is fixed.** Every persona lives in the autumn of 2004: San Andreas came out on 26 October, Burnout 3 in September, Halo 2 in November, The Sims 2 in September; Midtown Madness 3 (Xbox, 2003) is the latest in the series; Need for Speed Underground 2 lands in November. Nobody on the panel has seen a game made after December 2004. They judge the look against 2004 hardware, not against 2026.

**What the scale means.** A 1-10 scale anchored on real 2004-era review scores (verified on each game's Wikipedia Reception table, 6 Oct 2026; GameSpot and IGN themselves would not open for scripted reads):

| Anchor | Real score of the era | What it means on this panel |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Burnout 3: Takedown (GameSpot 9.5, Metacritic 93-94); GTA Vice City (GameSpot 9.6, IGN 9.7) | The best of its kind that year. You lose evenings to it. |
| 8 | Midtown Madness (1999; GameSpot 7.7, IGN 8.4, PC Gamer US 90%); Midtown Madness 2 (GameSpot 8.1, IGN 8.5); Simpsons Hit & Run (GameSpot 8.0-8.3) | A game people still remember fondly. Real flaws, real fun. |
| 7 | Midtown Madness 3 (Metacritic 76; critics split from Eurogamer 9 to Game Informer 5.25) | Good, forgettable for most, loved by some. |
| 4 | Driv3r (2004; GameSpot 3.8 PC, Edge 3/10, Metacritic 40-57) | Broken where it matters: stiff controls, nothing to do. |
| 2 | (no anchor game) | Unplayable or nothing works. |

A persona's score is the weighted mean of their criteria, each 1 to 10. **Rule: the anchors in each rubric describe THIS game**, so a reviewer never has to translate "what would a 5 look like" on their own.

## 1. Max Lavoie, 17, Aylmer

**Who.** Francophone, lives four streets from Chemin Fraser, has his apprentice permit and drives his father's '97 Grand Caravan on Saturdays. Played the Midtown Madness 2 demo (San Francisco) until the disc died, then bought the full game at Future Shop in the Galeries. On his PC in 2004: GTA Vice City (Hawaiian shirt mod), Need for Speed Underground, Counter-Strike 1.6 at Lan ETS once in Montreal, Unreal Tournament 2004. He knows every shortcut through the Galeries d'Aylmer parking lot and which dep sells to minors.

**What bores him.** Cards before he can drive. Slow speed limits. Missions that are "drive to the pillar, press E" five times. Anything that looks like a school project. Fake places where real ones should be.

**What he would tell friends (MSN Messenger, 23 h):** good: "man y'a la rue Principale pis tu peux sauter le dos-d'âne devant le Tim, c'est malade". Bad: "c'est Aylmer mais c'est vide, tu roules pis y se passe rien".

## The shared verdicts (every persona answers these three)

1. **After one hour, do I keep playing?** Yes or no, plus the minute that decided it (from the evidence timeline).
2. **Do I tell friends?** The exact sentence, in their voice and language.
3. **Cult classic?** On a 0-3 scale: 0 no; 1 "a few people would remember it"; 2 "my group would pass it around"; 3 "people would still talk about it in 2014". With the one thing that would move it up a point.

## What "100% there" means (the checklist every persona scores against)

The great city driving games of 2001-2005 had, between them (research for this panel, 6 Oct 2026; sources in METHOD.md):

1. A real city you recognise, with landmarks (Midtown Madness, Simpsons Hit & Run).
2. A free Cruise mode you can tune: traffic, pedestrians, weather, time (Midtown Madness 1 and 3).
3. Routes you choose and shortcuts that pay for knowing the map (Midnight Club II).
4. A one-glance core loop with a clock that rewards doing well (Crazy Taxi).
5. Risk for reward: near misses and stunts pay (Burnout 3).
6. Crashes worth watching (Burnout 3).
7. Police that escalate, and a cat-and-mouse mode (GTA's wanted stars, Midtown's Cops & Robbers).
8. A radio that gives the place its identity (Vice City, San Andreas).
9. Humour, and pedestrians who dive clear (Hit & Run, Midtown).
10. Varied missions and secrets: jumps, hidden things, unlocks (San Andreas).

Each persona marks each item **present / weak / missing** in this build, from the evidence. The panel's "100% there" score is the share marked present by at least three of five.
