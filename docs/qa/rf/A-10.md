## Result and implementation

Sector CPU generation now runs in a dedicated Worker. Teleports and direct vehicle resets hold simulation until the destination geometry, queries and first GPU submissions are ready. The same CPU bake boundary serves synchronous callers and the Worker; no world geometry or rendering budget was reduced.

The Worker reproduces main initialization: landmark mutations, Ottawa merge, 2004 rollback and footprint exclusions, followed by the effective material atlas. It transfers one mesh at a time, waits for its sequence acknowledgement and releases that builder before sending more. Exact camera buffers and indexed query data follow. Road queries preserve the service-road bit and streetsOnly argument from the street port.

The main-thread client owns incomplete uploads and frees them on errors or cancellation. A pending upload counts against the existing two-slice GPU ceiling. Readiness includes query hydration and actual mesh draw submission. Initial prewarm initializes the environment without advancing the clock, uses an identity transform and closes renderer frames even after a draw error. The prototype confirms the particular mesh through its after-render callback.

A newer teleport invalidates queued and active preparation. Stale completion cannot move the vehicle or release a replacement loading hold. Camera, capture, HUD and review tools await the same readiness contract. Ordinary resident teleports still place the vehicle synchronously before returning their resolved promise.

Reactive rebuild cleanup now returns the 13 per-kind prop meshes and three particle meshes, in addition to street chunks and pedestrian meshes. Shared handles are freed once. A counting-renderer reproduction grew from 31 to 79 live handles across three old rebuilds; the regression using the real constructor and main refresh function now proves bounded lifetime and retained score.
