## 1. Municipal Politics & The Amalgamation Wound

- **The 2002 Forced Merger (*Les fusions forcées*):** On January 1, 2002, the Parti Québécois government under Lucien Bouchard/Bernard Landry legally dissolved the independent historic town of Aylmer (incorporated 1847) along with Hull, Gatineau, Buckingham, and Masson-Angers, forming the single amalgamated mega-city of *Ville de Gatineau*.
- **The Local Sentiment in Summer 2004:** In summer 2004, the merger was only two and a half years old, and the wound was raw. Nobody who grew up in Aylmer said they lived in "Gatineau". Calling an Aylmer native a "Gatinois" was an insult.
- **The Demerger Referendum (June 20, 2004):** Just weeks before the game begins, on Sunday June 20, 2004, Québec held provincial demerger referendums. Aylmer voted overwhelmingly to separate from Gatineau, but narrowly failed to reach the required provincial voter-turnout threshold (35% of registered electors). The anger and bitter resignation were fresh topics of conversation on every porch and garage driveway that summer.
- **Visual Artifacts:** Old "Ville d'Aylmer" crests still painted on public works trucks, fire hydrants, and arena signage. Blue and green municipal recycling bins carrying different logos depending on which year they were issued.

## 2. Gas Prices & The Teenage Economy

- **Price at the Pump:** In July and August 2004, regular unleaded gasoline at the Petro-Canada (Rue Principale) and Ultramar fluctuated between **$0.819 and $0.889 per litre** ($3.10 to $3.35 CAD per US gallon).
- **The Teen Budget:** A $20 bill (the green polymer-free paper Canadian banknote with Queen Elizabeth II) bought ~23.5 litres of 87 octane, which was more than half a tank in the Ranger's 57-litre fuel tank.
- **Minimum Wage:** Québec minimum wage in summer 2004 was **$7.45/hr** (having increased from $7.30 on May 1, 2004). Working a 7-hour shift at a local chip truck or grocery store yielded about $45 after taxes—enough for a tank of gas, a pack of Export 'A', and two large poutines.
- **Canadian Tire Money:** Paper Canadian Tire money was omnipresent. Every glovebox, center console, and bedroom dresser had a thick paper-clipped stack of 5¢, 10¢, and 25¢ scrip with Sandy McTire's smiling Scottish face.

## 3. Summer 2004 Soundtrack & Radio Airwaves

### The Definitive Québec & Outaouais Summer Anthems
1. **Les Trois Accords — *Hawaïenne* & *Saskatchewan*:** The album *Gros mammouth album turbo* exploded across Québec radio in spring/summer 2004. Every teen with a guitar was playing the four chords to *Hawaïenne*.
2. **K-Maro — *Femme Like U*:** Released in spring 2004, this Montreal hip-hop/R&B track became an inescapable cultural phenomenon across French Canada, playing from rolled-down windows at every red light.
3. **Simple Plan — *Don't Wanna Think About You* & *Welcome to My Life*:** Montreal pop-punk heroes; ubiquitous on MusiquePlus.
4. **Éric Lapointe — *Coup d'joker* / *N'importe quoi*:** The staple soundtrack of suburban garage mechanics and woodstove bonfires.
5. **English Pop/Rock Hits of Summer 2004:** Usher (*Yeah!*), Hoobastank (*The Reason*), Maroon 5 (*This Love*, *She Will Be Loved*), OutKast (*Hey Ya!*), Franz Ferdinand (*Take Me Out*), Blink-182 (*I Miss You*), Modest Mouse (*Float On*).

### The Outaouais Radio Dial
- **CHEZ 106 (106.1 FM):** Ottawa classic rock (Led Zeppelin, Rush, Pink Floyd, The Tragically Hip). The station of dads working in garages and carpenters on job sites.
- **CKTF 104.1 FM (Énergie Outaouais):** The French commercial teen hit station. Fast-talking DJs, dance hits, K-Maro, rock québécois.
- **CIMF 94.9 FM (RockDétente Outaouais):** Softer adult contemporary playing in dentist offices and grocery stores.
- **CHUO 89.1 FM:** University of Ottawa bilingual campus radio (indie rock, hip-hop, electronic).
- **CKCU 93.1 FM:** Carleton University community radio (eclectic, folk, late-night experimental).
- **The Bear 106.9 FM (CKQB):** Ottawa's hard rock / active rock station (Nickelback, Velvet Revolver, Metallica).

## 4. Technology & Material Culture in 2004

- **No Smartphones:** The iPhone would not exist until 2007. Mobile phones were flip phones (Nokia 3310, Motorola V60, Sanyo flip phones on Telus Mobility or Bell Mobility) with monochromatic or primitive 4,096-color screens and monochrome ringtones. Texting was billed at 15¢ per message or 250 texts/month plans; teenagers mostly used payphones or landlines.
- **MSN Messenger (Windows Live):** The primary social network. You spent the day driving around town, then logged on at 11:00 PM to chat with classmates using nudge sounds, custom emoticon packs, and personal status messages quoting Blink-182 lyrics.
- **Burning CDs:** Nero Burning ROM, Adaptec Easy CD Creator, or Windows Media Player 9. You downloaded MP3s on Kazaa Lite, LimeWire, or Soulseek over a 1.5 Mbps Sympatico DSL connection, burned a 700 MB / 80-minute Memorex CD-R, and labeled it with a black Sharpie.
- **Portable Audio:** Sony Discman with "G-Protection" (45-second anti-skip buffer) plugged into the car's cigarette lighter via a 12V adapter and a cassette tape audio adapter with a dangling black wire.
- **Renting Movies:** Visiting the Rogers Video or local dep on Friday night to rent DVD or VHS copies of *The Fast and the Furious*, *EuroTrip*, or *Kill Bill Vol. 2*.

## 5. Sports & The Looming NHL Lockout

- **The Ottawa Senators Heartbreak:** In the spring 2004 playoffs, the Ottawa Senators were eliminated in Game 7 of the Eastern Conference Quarterfinals by the Toronto Maple Leafs (Joe Nieuwendyk scoring two soft goals on Patrick Lalime). Lalime was traded to St. Louis in June 2004, leaving fans fiercely debating the goaltending future.
- **The Looming Lockout:** Throughout summer 2004, sports radio (The Team 1200) and bar chatter was dominated by the impending NHL collective bargaining expiration. On September 15, 2004, NHL commissioner Gary Bettman officially locked out the players, cancelling the entire 2004–2005 NHL season. The summer of 2004 was the last time anyone saw NHL hockey for over a year.

## 6. Road Infrastructure & Geography

- **Boulevard des Allumettières Construction:** In 2004, the major four-lane bypass connecting Aylmer to Hull (Highway 148 / Boulevard des Allumettières) was in the middle of active phased construction. Heavy earthmovers, fresh blacktop, detour signs, and raw gravel cuts were common sights.
- **The Champlain Bridge Cycling Path:** Following the NCC's 2002 rehabilitation and three-lane widening project, the Champlain Bridge had an active, separated cycling path on the east side connecting the Voyageurs Pathway to Ottawa's Island Park Drive and Wellington Street network.
- **The Old Rail Line:** The decommissioned CP Rail line cutting through Fraser and Aylmer was already converted to a multi-use gravel path, serving as the hidden trail network for teenagers on mountain bikes avoiding the police on Principale.
